    Why it exists

    Star averages say how people feel. They don't say why.

    Reading a few hundred reviews by hand can take most of a day and often ends in a list of anecdotes. This skill labels every review it fetches, counts problems by product area, and shows which ranks hold up when the sample is resampled and which could easily swap.

    What you get

    A report your product team can act on

    A finished run produces one self-contained report.html. Claude opens it in your browser and gives you a link to it.

    The top of the Todoist sample report. Open the full report ↗

    The sample report uses public Google Play reviews of Todoist, shown without reviewer names. To have a review removed, open an issue.

    • Fix first

      A short summary that names the top three areas, each linked to the reviews behind it.

    • Ranked product areas

      Issue counts, blocking problems, churn and praise per area, plus a likely-rank range from resampling, so you can see which ranks are stable and which could swap.

    • Verbatim quotes

      Each quote is a sentence picked from the review itself, shortened with … when it's long. Quotes are never paraphrased.

    • Bugs, requests, and churn

      Bug reports with repro detail, feature requests, people saying they'll leave, and problems that started with an update.

    • Trends and versions

      An area is marked "fewer" or "more lately" only when a statistical test suggests the change is unlikely to be chance alone. Versions are listed newest first.

    How it works

    Each part does what it's good at

    CodeFetches and counts

    Python scripts fetch the reviews, count, rank, and render the report. Star ratings, dates, and versions aren't sent to Jev; they're joined back in code.

    JevLabels every review

    TypeSafe's Jev model reads each review once and answers about 45 narrow questions in parallel (the count grows with the number of product areas):

    • sentiment and severity
    • a problem and a praise question for each product area
    • bug, feature request, churn, "since an update"
    • which of the review's own sentences to quote

    ClaudeTailors and explains

    Claude drafts product areas for your app ("Free-tier limits", "Editor and typing"), checks the results, and writes the summary from the computed numbers and quotes.

    Quotes are picked from the reviews, never written by a model. Claude writes the summary from the computed numbers, so check it against the tables. Near 50/50 labels are left out of the counts and listed for a person to check.

    1. Fetch the reviews from your linkCode
    2. Draft 10–20 product areas for this appClaude
    3. Label every reviewJev
    4. Count, rank, and resample for rank rangesCode
    5. Check the results; widen areas and rerun if neededClaude
    6. Write the summary from the numbersClaude
    7. Render the reportCode
    For researchers

    How the numbers are made

    The rules behind every count and rank, so you can explain them in a readout. Every Jev question, threshold, and weight is in one file, jev_questions.py, where you can check or change them.

    When a label counts
    Jev gives a probability for each question. A label counts at 60% or higher. Between 40% and 60% it's left out of the counts and listed as borderline for a person to check. Off-topic needs 75%, because it removes a review from every count.
    Severity
    Minor: cosmetic, or a dislike of a design or business decision. Degraded: something works poorly, or a feature is missing or was removed. Blocking: the app or the reviewer's main task doesn't work at all, or they lost data or money.
    Priority
    Each problem review in an area adds 1 + severity (0–3) + 2 if the reviewer mentions leaving + 1 if they tie it to an update. Areas are ranked by that sum, so a few blocking reviews can outrank many minor ones.
    Likely rank
    The reviews are resampled 500 times with a fixed seed, so reruns match. "Likely #1–3" is the middle 90% of the ranks an area gets. When two ranges overlap, their order isn't settled.
    Trends
    Reviews are split at the median date. An area is marked "fewer" or "more lately" only when a one-sided Fisher exact test gives p < 0.025, with at least 6 complaints. Monthly counts are shown either way.
    Counts and shares
    One review can count toward several areas, so area counts add up to more than the number of reviews that report a problem.

    What we've tested

    • Repeatability. We reran Jev 5 times on the same 300 reviews for each of 3 apps. Between 0.2% and 0.4% of label decisions changed, and the top 3 areas held (one app swapped #2 and #3).
    • Sampling noise. With 300 reviews, only the top 2 to 4 areas were reliably ordered. At 10,000, the top 4 rank ranges were one or two places wide.

    What we haven't tested

    • Agreement with human coders. No study yet compares Jev's labels with a researcher's codes. Spot-check the quotes and borderline reviews before you cite a number.
    • Who writes reviews. Reviewers choose themselves and lean toward strong feelings. The report describes these reviewers, not your whole user base.
    Get started

    Set it up once, then just paste a link

    You need Claude Code and a TypeSafe API key (TypeSafe bills per use; see how many reviews for estimates). You don't write code. Setup is four steps: paste each command into a terminal (on a Mac, the Terminal app) and press Return.

    Set up once usually about 5 minutes

    1. Clone the skill into your skills folder

      git clone https://github.com/Ying8109/app-review-triage.git ~/.claude/skills/app-review-triage

      If your Mac asks to install developer tools, say yes; that installs git. For one project only, clone into that project's .claude/skills/app-review-triage instead.

    2. Install uv

      uv runs the scripts and installs their Python dependencies on first use.

      curl -LsSf https://astral.sh/uv/install.sh | sh

      Or brew install uv. On Windows, or without uv, see the README's install steps.

    3. Add your TypeSafe API key to your shell profile

      Create a key at console.typesafe.ai/keys, then add this line to ~/.zshrc or ~/.bashrc:

      export TYPESAFE_API_KEY="your-key-here"

      Replace your-key-here with your key. On Windows, run setx TYPESAFE_API_KEY "your-key-here" instead. Never paste the key into a chat or commit it to a repository.

    4. Start a new Claude Code session

      Open a new terminal so it sees the key, and start Claude Code. In the Claude desktop app, start a new session. The skill is picked up automatically.

    Each run usually a few minutes for 300 reviews

    1. Paste a review link

      Ask in your own words, or type /app-review-triage.

    2. Answer four questions

      Whether your key is set (never the key itself), which product and link, what to categorize, and how many reviews. For categories, Claude can draft product areas from the reviews, use the areas your team tracks, or shape them around one research question. It repeats your answers back before fetching anything.

    3. Approve the run plan

      After fetching, Claude shows the reviews' date range, the product areas Jev will be asked about, what text goes to TypeSafe and Anthropic, and the estimated time and cost. Nothing goes to Jev until you say "run it".

    4. Open the report

      Claude opens report.html in your browser and recaps what you asked for against what was analyzed. Want different areas? Ask: a rerun asks Jev only about the areas you changed.

    Trust and privacy

    What leaves your machine, and what doesn't

    Sent to TypeSafe and Anthropic

    Each review's title and text, plus the app's name, go to TypeSafe's API for labeling; star ratings, dates, and versions aren't sent to it. Claude also reads a sample of reviews and a summary of the results while it works, so that text goes to Anthropic as part of your Claude Code session.

    Your API key

    The TypeSafe SDK reads it from your environment. No script prints it or writes it to a file, and the skill tells Claude never to ask for the key itself.

    Reviews are treated as untrusted

    • Every field is escaped in the report, and its security policy lets only its own script run.
    • CSV cells that start like a spreadsheet formula get a leading apostrophe, so spreadsheets show them as text.
    • The skill tells Claude to treat reviews as data and not to follow instructions inside them. This lowers the risk from a malicious review but can't rule it out.

    Before you share a report

    It contains every analyzed review in full, and a reviewer may have included an email address or order number. Private exports such as support tickets are sent to TypeSafe and appear in the report too.

    Limits and questions

    Good to know

    Do I need to write code?
    No. Claude runs the scripts. You run a few setup commands once to install the skill and uv and add your TypeSafe key.
    What does a run cost?
    In our tests, Jev cost roughly $0.06 to $0.10 per 300 reviews. Treat that as an estimate: longer reviews and more product areas cost more, and TypeSafe sets its own prices. Claude's own steps count toward your usual Claude Code usage.
    How accurate are the labels?
    Repeat runs agree closely, but no study yet compares the labels with human coders. Near 50/50 labels are left out of the counts and listed for a person to check, and each rank comes with a likely range. See How the numbers are made.
    Can I put these numbers in a readout?
    Yes, as a triage of what reviewers say. Quote the rank range with each rank, check the quotes you use against the review, and say the sample is public reviewers, not all users.
    Which languages work?
    English works best. Other languages are labeled but flagged in the report.
    Can I use my own product areas?
    Yes. Name your features or areas and the skill tells Claude to use them as you wrote them, adding only what's missing.
    What about review sites like Trustpilot?
    Pages with structured review data often work directly. For others, Claude can try reading the page in a browser, but some sites block this or show only a few hundred reviews.
